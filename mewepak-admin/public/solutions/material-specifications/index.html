Understand your structure

Every layer
has a purpose.

Thin films use two layers. Medium and Thick films use three. The complete structure determines the material’s performance.

01 / OUTER LAMINATEThe first impression

BOPP or PET, with a gloss or matte surface.

02 / PRINT SURFACEThe visual foundation

PET or metallized PET in three-layer structures.

03 / INNER SEALANTThe sealing layer

Structure-specific mLLDPE or EVOH PE.

01 / GAUGE

Film thickness

Review both mil and micron values alongside handling and sealing requirements. All listed structures have a ±10% thickness tolerance.

02 / OXYGEN

Oxygen transmission

OTR describes oxygen passing through the film. Compare the reference value for each complete structure, not just the outer film.

03 / MOISTURE

Water vapor transmission

WVTR describes moisture passing through the film. Review it together with OTR and the product’s storage requirements.

04 / SEALING

Seal performance

Check the temperature range and self-to-self seal strength. Thin structures use a different temperature range from Medium and Thick films.

A little more clarity

Material questions, answered.

How do Thin, Medium and Thick structures differ?

Thin options are two-layer BOPP films at 2.4 or 2.7 mil. Medium options are three-layer BOPP films at 3.1 or 3.4 mil. Thick options are three-layer BOPP films at 4.6 or 4.9 mil, plus three-layer gloss PET options at 4.8 mil. Compare individual barrier and sealing values before choosing.

What is the difference between Clear, White and Metallized?

The guide describes Clear options with clear or frosted product windows, White options with a white interior, and Metallized options with a silver interior and metallic artwork effects. The complete laminate varies by material, so review the film construction as well as appearance.

Are these values an approved production specification?

The supplied material sheets are marked “For review.” All values on this page are reference values, subject to MewePak technical approval. Ask the team to confirm the structure and requirements for your application before production.

Does a 12-month film shelf life mean my product will last 12 months?

No. The reference is the film’s shelf life from delivery under the specified storage conditions. Filled-product shelf life needs a separate assessment for the product and its packaging application.

        The bigger picture

        What makes packaging
        more sustainable?

        It starts with a pack that protects what is inside. From there, consider material use, the customer experience and what can happen to the packaging after use.

        Environmental goals sit alongside product safety, practical performance and commercial needs. A useful comparison looks at the complete life of the pack.

        Pouch advantages

        Small details.
        Meaningful possibilities.

        A stand-up pouch brings several design choices into one format. Explore the opportunities, then assess them against the pack you use today.

        4 packaging considerations

        • Use material thoughtfully

          Compare the weight of the complete pouch with your current pack. Confirm that any reduction still meets handling and protection needs.

        • Plan for transport

          Review empty-pack dimensions, case quantities and pallet layout. Compare freight using the same delivery assumptions.

        • Protect product quality

          Choose a barrier and seal system around the product. Shelf-life testing helps establish how the proposed pack performs.

        • Make room for your brand

          Explore sizes, print finishes and opening features that suit the shelf, the product and the way customers use it.

        The appropriate format depends on the product, its protection needs, the supply chain and the recovery systems available in your market.

        Performance matters

        Good intentions.
        Practical questions.

        A material change has to work for the product, your operation and the people who use the pack. Bring these questions into the conversation early.

        Explore packaging FAQs
        Will the product stay protected?

        Start with moisture, oxygen, light and storage requirements. Compare the proposed structure with the current pack and agree the testing needed to validate shelf life and product quality.

        Will it work on our filling line?

        Share the equipment, filling conditions, seal settings and line speed requirements. Sample trials can help assess how the selected film or pouch behaves before a production decision.

        How will the cost compare?

        Compare the complete specification, quantity, artwork and delivery assumptions. Include any changes to filling, storage and handling in the commercial discussion.

        How do we support the claim?

        Request material specifications, relevant assessment reports and current certificates. Confirm that the evidence covers the complete pack and the market where it will be used.

        Method, assumptions and reading

        Packaging mass is the number of packs multiplied by grams per pack, divided by 1,000. Each stage’s emissions are packaging mass multiplied by its entered factor in kg CO₂e per kg of finished packaging. Stage totals are then added.

        Use consistent boundaries, geography, time period and data quality for both packs. Enter each activity in one stage to avoid double counting. Factors here represent gross emissions. Document any recovery credits and additional impacts in a project assessment.

        The default factors are illustrative values for exploring the tool. A full LCA requires a defined goal and scope, inventory data, impact assessment and interpretation.

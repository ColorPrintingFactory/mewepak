What if I am not sure which format I need?

Choose “Please advise” in your enquiry. Tell us about your product, intended fill quantity and how the packaging will be used. A photograph of an existing pack can also help explain your starting point.

Which details should I include in my brief?

Start with the product, approximate quantity and any known dimensions. Add your filling or storage requirements, preferred opening style and the artwork or finish you have in mind.

How do I share artwork and reference files?

Use the form to prepare and review your brief, then open the email draft. Attach your artwork, photographs or reference documents in your email app before sending.

    A practical place to start

    Eco Packaging Guide

    Start with your product, then ask how the pack will be used and what happens afterwards. Use these four conversations to shape your next packaging brief.

    01

    Reduce the unnecessary.

    Ask whether the pouch can be right-sized, whether the fill quantity needs extra headspace and which features serve a real purpose. Check the proposed pack with your actual product before changing a specification.

    02

    Make reuse practical.

    If refill or repeated use is part of your plan, describe how the customer will open, close, clean or refill the pack. Discuss the product, container and handling needs together rather than choosing a closure in isolation.

    03

    Check the next step.

    Ask for the full material structure and the disposal guidance intended for the markets where you sell. Check which collection and treatment routes customers can actually access before deciding what to print on the pack.

    04

    Request the evidence.

    Ask what supports a proposed material or environmental claim, which components it covers and what testing is needed. Confirm the wording, symbols and customer instructions alongside the final specification and artwork.

    Bring these details to the conversation.

    • Product, fill weight and intended shelf life
    • Filling, sealing, storage and transport needs
    • Customer use and intended disposal market
    • Material details, supporting evidence and artwork wording

    Final material suitability, claims and disposal instructions are checked for the specific pack and selling market.
